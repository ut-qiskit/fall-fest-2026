Come to all of it or pick one. Each part stands on its own.

Questions

Do I need to know quantum mechanics?

No. The first problems assume nothing beyond some Python, and IBM publishes free Qiskit tutorials that cover everything the challenge uses. Links go out with your registration confirmation.

Do I have to do all of it?

No. The three parts are independent. You can present a poster without touching the challenge, do the challenge without coming to campus, or just show up Friday for the talk.

Can I take part remotely?

The challenge is fully online, so yes. The poster session is in person on campus. Whether the Friday talk is streamed depends on the speaker's format, and we will post the answer here once IBM confirms it.

Can I present a poster as an undergraduate?

Yes. Undergraduate and graduate researchers are both welcome, and work in progress is fine. A poster session is a good place to get feedback before results are finished.

Does my poster have to be about quantum computing?

Quantum information, quantum materials, and nanoscale devices are all in scope. If you are unsure whether your work fits, email us and we will tell you.

What is Qiskit Fall Fest?

A season of quantum computing events held each autumn at universities around the world, organized by students and supported by IBM Quantum. Hundreds of campuses take part. This is the UT Austin one.
